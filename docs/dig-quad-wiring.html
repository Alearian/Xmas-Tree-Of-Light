Dig-Quad connection diagram

How the controller, the two 12 V supplies and the 12 WS2815 strips connect. The Dig-Quad sends data only. Strip power goes from each PSU through its own fuse block, never through the board.

Before anything is bought or switched on
  • This uses the working assumption of 4 A per 6 m strip (The builder's figure). One source says 7.2 A. Measure one strip at full white first. Above about 4.5 A, change to 3 PSUs with 4 strips each.
  • Mains wiring to the PSUs, and any relay that switches mains, must be done by a qualified electrician.
  • Terminal positions are drawn schematically. Use the labels printed on the board and on the strip pads.

1 · Whole system

Two supplies, each with its own fuse block. PSU 1 also runs the Dig-Quad through a small 2 A fuse. Every negative terminal meets at one ground bar.

+12 V GND (0 V) Data Backup data (BI/BO) Mains AC ● joined   ⌒ hop = crossing, not joined

2 · One output in detail (LED1 → strips 1, 2, 3)

All four outputs are wired the same way. LED2 drives strips 4–6, LED3 drives 7–9 and LED4 drives 10–12. Data snakes up, down and up. Power goes into both ends of every strip, each feed with its own 5 A fuse.

Pads are drawn as + D B G (+12 V, data, backup, GND) reading along a strip that points up. A strip that points down is rotated, so its pad order is mirrored. Check the labels printed on the real strip, because the order varies between makers.

Why it's wired this way

Data needs a shared 0 V

The data line is a voltage measured against ground. If the strip's GND and the board's GND aren't connected, the strip can't read the signal. That's why every negative goes to one bar and why each data wire has a GND wire beside it.

Join grounds, never V+

Two supplies never output exactly 12.00 V. Joining their V+ makes one push current into the other. Joining their grounds is safe and gives everything the same reference.

Power bypasses the board

Each PSU feeds about 24 A. The Dig-Quad is rated 30 A for the whole board and 10 A per fuse, and takes only one supply, so the strips are fed from fuse blocks instead.

Feeding both ends

A 6 m strip loses voltage along its thin copper. Feeding the top and the base halves the current in each half, which keeps the far LEDs from going dim and orange at full white.

A fuse protects the wire

Each fuse is sized for the thinnest wire after it. A 5 A fuse on an 18 AWG pigtail blows before the wire overheats if a strip end shorts.

BI and the backup line

WS2815 has a second data line. If one pixel dies, the next one reads BI instead and the rest of the strip keeps working. At the first pixel there's nothing before it, so BI is tied to GND.
